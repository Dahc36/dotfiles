# Honest shape

A reader infers the structure of code from its surface before reading any of it.
Two different calls read as two different sources, two names as two things, a symmetry as a parallel.
The shape of the code should only suggest structure that is really there.

## A difference in form claims a difference in substance

When one thing is reached, named or written two ways in one unit, the reader looks for the difference that justifies it and finds none.
One thing takes one form within a unit; a second form is justified only by a second thing.
An indirection earns its place where it names something for readers outside; inside, beside the source itself, it claims a second source.

## Sameness in form claims sameness in substance

Two things written alike are read as the same kind of thing.
When they differ, that difference belongs in the shape, not in a detail the reader has to find.
