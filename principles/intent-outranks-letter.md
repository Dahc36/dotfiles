# Intent outranks the letter

What was agreed is written before the work exists, by someone who could not see what doing it would find.
Its sentences are the best wording of an intent at that time, not the intent itself.

## The goals say what a sentence was for

When following a sentence would defeat the goal it was written to serve, the sentence is what is wrong.
The choice that serves the goal is the one its author would have made, had they known.

## The letter stands until it conflicts

A sentence that merely looks improvable still says what was agreed.
Only a conflict with the goals overrides it; a preference does not.

## Intent has bounds

The intent is what was stated as goals and non-goals, not what the author can be imagined to want.
A choice that reaches past them serves a different agreement.
